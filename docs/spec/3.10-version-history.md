### 3.10 Version history

Adam, 2026-11-16, by voice: "the version history feature should be used
in our other apps also, such as vizi, wushroom, acuteping"; 2026-11-26
14:57, in chat: "Can you do version history?" (PBI-2.24). The feature
is the Snake demo's (`sugose/snake-demo`, `docs/SPEC.md` 2.19), whose
item (1) Adam decided for that app; item 1 below carries it over. That
app is a web page in English that uses the browser's storage. This one
is a phone app in three languages with a first-start disclaimer, so
items 2 to 12 are written for it.

In this section **[Clead]** marks Clead's proposals of 2026-11-26
(#131), which Adam had not confirmed when they were written; a mark at
the head of an item covers the item. **Open (Adam)** marks a question
that is his and is not decided, with Clead's recommendation; the text
says what holds until he decides. No code is written from this section
before his approval (product-office `RULES.md` § 29 (3)).

1. **Version history** (Adam, as he decided it for the model). The app
   has a history of its versions. A "Version history" link opens it.
   The history opens by itself the first time the app is started after
   a version upgrade, and shows what is new. Its text is a list written
   for the app's users, one entry for each version, in plain words,
   built into the app; it is not `CHANGELOG.md`. After an upgrade only
   the versions newer than the last one this phone saw are shown, with
   a button to the full history. On a first start, with no stored
   version, nothing opens; the link is always there.
2. **The link** (**[Clead]**, 2026-11-26, #131). The link is on the
   About screen, first on the screen, above the app's licence; it is a
   link to a screen reader, as the link to About is. It is in About and
   not in Settings because Settings holds what the user sets (the key,
   the language, the model, the lookups' starting values; 3.1, 3.9) and
   one link, to About, the screen where the app tells about itself (its
   licence, the disclaimer, the credits, the libraries). The history is
   of that kind. It stands first there because the lists below it are
   long.
3. **The screen** (**[Clead]**, 2026-11-26, #131). The history is one
   screen of the app, as About is: its title is in the header, in the
   app's language; it keeps clear of the system bars and the display
   cutout as every screen does (PBI-2.20); it scrolls when its text
   does not fit, and never sideways; the header's back control and the
   phone's back close it and show the screen under it. It is a screen,
   not a system alert (as 3.1 has a question asked on the screen). Its
   headings are headings to a screen reader and its button a button,
   as on About and Settings, and its text follows the phone's text size
   and its light or dark setting as the other screens' does. It holds
   back nothing: the queue runs as 3.4 says while it is shown.
   - Opened from the link, its title is "Version history" and it shows
     every entry, newest first.
   - Opened by itself (item 7), its title is "What's new", it shows the
     entries item 7 names, newest first, and below them a button "Show
     full history". Pressing the button shows every entry under the
     title "Version history" and removes the button. It stays the same
     screen: back still shows the screen under it.
   - An entry is shown as a heading and its lines. The heading holds
     the word for "Version" in the app's language, the version, and the
     entry's date written in the app's language as a pin's date on the
     map is (3.9, The Library). The lines are those of the app's
     language (item 9), as a list in the order of the data, shown as
     plain text.
   - The screen shows only text built into the app. Opened by an
     address from outside the app, it is the screen opened from the
     link, and nothing stored changes.
4. **The entries** (**[Clead]**, 2026-11-26, #131). The list is data in
   a module of its own (section 2, `history`), newest first. Nothing of
   it is read from the network, a file or the store when the app runs.
   An entry has a version (item 5), a date written `YYYY-MM-DD` that is
   a real calendar date (`2026-02-30` is not), and for each of the
   app's three languages one or more lines, each a text that is not
   empty and not only spaces. The three languages have the same number
   of lines, line for line. No two entries have the same version, and
   the list is in descending order of version. The version of
   `app.json` has an entry, and it is the first, so a version cannot be
   set without writing what it brings; a Jest test checks it. The
   screen's own texts (the two titles, the link, the button, the word
   for "Version") are strings of the i18n module, as any other.
   **Open (Adam):** whether the list also holds the versions from
   before the history. `app.json` has held 0.1.0 (until #99), 0.2.0
   (#99; tagged `v0.2.0`) and 0.2.1 (#117; not built, by `STATUS.md`
   of 2026-11-26); which version is the first with the history is not
   decided either. **[Clead]** recommends an entry for each earlier
   version that was installed on a phone, written from `CHANGELOG.md`
   in the user's words, so that the full history is full. Until he
   decides, the list holds the build's version and may begin there.
5. **Versions** (**[Clead]**, 2026-11-26, #131). A version is three
   whole numbers joined by dots, each written without a leading zero
   (`0.2.1`, `1.10.0`), and nothing else: no `v`, no space, no
   prerelease or build part. Two versions are compared number by
   number, as numbers of any length, and the first difference decides
   (`0.9.0` is lower than `0.10.0`). This is the part of the model's
   rule (SemVer 2.0.0) that the app's versions have used; it is simpler
   and needs no expression taken from semver.org. A version of
   `app.json` in another form fails the test of item 4. The build's
   version, V below, is the `version` of `app.json` as built into the
   app, read as the Wikimedia User-Agent reads it (`expo-constants`;
   `src/picture/devicePicture.ts`; Known unknown 17). A new build with
   the same `version` is not an upgrade, whatever its Android
   `versionCode`.
6. **What is stored** (**[Clead]**, 2026-11-26, #131). The version last
   seen is kept in the app's plain key-value store, the one that holds
   the disclaimer's acceptance and the language choice
   (`src/storage/keyValueStore.ts`), under a key of its own,
   `history.seen-version`, as the version's text. It is not a secret
   and never goes in the secure store. Read on `main` at `41d833d` for
   this item:
   - the schema version and its one-way migrations (3.9, The store) are
     the SQLite database's (`PRAGMA user_version`,
     `src/store/schema.ts`);
   - the key-value store has no version. Each of its keys is read by
     its own module, which takes a missing or unexpected value as no
     value (`src/disclaimer/acceptance.ts`,
     `src/i18n/languageChoice.ts`, `src/key/apiKey.ts`);
   - the app stores no version of itself there today.

   So the new key needs no migration and changes no schema version, and
   a build without the history never reads it. The store is kept out of
   the phone's backups (3.6) and is deleted with the app (the install
   marker of 3.2 rests on that), so the first start after a reinstall
   is a first start. Once a build with the key is on a phone, the key's
   name and the form of its value are not changed; a later form is read
   beside this one.
7. **What opens by itself** (**[Clead]**, 2026-11-26, #131). The
   decision is made once at each start of the app (when the app's
   process starts, not when the app comes back to the front), after the
   disclaimer (item 8). With S the stored text and V the build's
   version:
   - S is missing: nothing opens (item 1), and V is written.
     **Open (Adam):** the builds before the history stored no version
     (item 6). On a phone that has one of them, the first build with
     the history finds none and, by item 1, shows nothing by itself,
     though for that phone it is a version upgrade; what is new is
     first shown there at the upgrade after that one. **[Clead]**
     recommends telling such a phone from a new install by the
     disclaimer: where its acceptance was already stored when this
     start read it (3.1), the phone has had the app before, and the
     screen opens by itself with the entry of V alone and the button;
     where the disclaimer is accepted at this start, nothing opens.
     The price is one more case in the decision, kept for good, for
     the phones that hold an earlier build. Until he decides, item 1
     holds: nothing opens.
   - S is not a version (item 5), or reading it fails: nothing opens.
     V is written; after a failed read the write is still tried.
   - S is V: nothing opens, and nothing is written.
   - S is higher than V (a build older than the one this phone saw
     last): nothing opens, and V is written.
   - S is lower than V: the screen opens by itself with the entries of
     the versions higher than S and not higher than V, newest first,
     and V is written. An upgrade that skipped versions shows the
     entries of all of them, each under its heading; there is no limit
     to their number, as the screen scrolls. With no such entry,
     nothing opens; the list as built always has one (item 4), so this
     is a case of the function only.
   - V cannot be read, or is not a version: nothing opens and nothing
     is written. The test of item 4 keeps `app.json`'s version valid,
     so only a failed read on a phone gives this.

   V is written when the decision is made, whatever the user then does
   with the screen. A write that fails is ignored, and the history may
   then open again at the next start. So while the store works, a
   version's news opens by itself at most once on a phone. The read
   of S is finished before the Library is shown, as the read of the
   disclaimer's acceptance is, from the same store; it has no time
   limit of its own, as that read has none. The history is then shown
   over the Library, and back shows the Library.
8. **The disclaimer, a share and the Library** (**[Clead]**,
   2026-11-26, #131). The first-start disclaimer (3.1) comes first,
   always: nothing of item 7 is decided, shown or written before it is
   accepted, as the queue does not run and a share is not read before
   then (3.9; `src/app/_layout.tsx`). On a new install the disclaimer
   is shown and accepted, then the Library; nothing opens by itself,
   and V is written. If the disclaimer is asked for again after an
   upgrade (its acceptance could not be stored), it is shown first and
   "What's new" once it is accepted. The screen opens by itself only
   at a start whose first screen is the Library. At a start that a
   share opens, the share's choice is the first screen (3.9, Shared
   photos): nothing opens by itself, no decision is made and nothing
   is written, so the decision falls to the next start that opens on
   the Library, which can be days later while Android keeps the app's
   process (**[not checked]** that the app can tell such a start;
   Known unknown 17). A share that arrives while the history is shown
   opens its choice over it, as over any screen (3.9).
9. **Languages** (**[Clead]**, 2026-11-26, #131). The history is shown
   in the app's language, as every screen is; changing the language in
   Settings changes it. A build always has every entry in all three
   languages: the test of item 4 fails otherwise. For the function
   alone: an entry without lines in the app's language is shown with
   its English lines, whole, not line by line, English being the
   language the app falls back to (3.1); an entry without English
   lines is not a valid entry. A Swedish or Italian text that no
   speaker has checked yet is still shown in that language, as the
   app's other strings are.
10. **Writing and checking an entry** (**[Clead]**, 2026-11-26, #131).
    An entry is written in the PR that sets its version in `app.json`
    (as #99 and #117 set theirs), and its date is that PR's. It says
    in plain words what the user can now do or what has changed for
    them: no PR or backlog numbers, no names from the code, and not
    the wording of `CHANGELOG.md`, which is the developers' record.
    The PR holds the entry in English, Swedish and Italian. Adam
    approves the English and the Swedish before the build of that
    version is made. The Italian is checked by the Italian speaker
    Adam names, as the disclaimer's was (front matter). Which entries'
    Swedish or Italian no speaker has checked is recorded beside the
    data, as the i18n module records it for its strings
    (`NOT_CHECKED_BY_A_SPEAKER`), and an entry leaves the record when
    its speaker has checked it. An entry's text may be corrected in a
    later build; its version and date stay.
    **Open (Adam):** whether a build waits for the Italian check.
    **[Clead]** recommends that it does not: the entry is recorded as
    not checked, shown as written (item 9) and corrected in a later
    build if the speaker finds a fault, since a wrong word in a list
    of news misleads nobody about a mushroom, unlike the disclaimer.
    Until he decides, he is asked at each such build.
11. **Out of scope** (**[Clead]**, 2026-11-26, #131).
    - A version history shared between Adam's apps, as code or as a
      project of its own. **Open (Adam):** PBI-2.24 leaves undecided
      whether the logic is shared code or a copy. **[Clead]**
      recommends a copy for now, written for this app from the model.
      Checked on 2026-11-26: the model's code is part of the Snake
      demo (`src/history.ts`), not a package; vizi has no version
      history yet (its PBI-1.40 is `LATER`); acuteping's entry was not
      read. What the two apps would share is the comparison of
      versions and the choice of entries, a few small functions. The
      rest differs: the storage (the browser's, which answers at
      once; here a store that answers later), the panel (HTML; here a
      screen), the languages (one; here three, with their checks),
      the version rule (item 5) and the order at start (item 8). A
      shared package needs a repository and releases of its own, which
      is more than those functions. Once two or three apps have their
      own, what they share is known from use and can be drawn out
      then. This section holds either way: it says what the app does,
      and where the code lives changes none of it.
    - News fetched from a server, a notice that a newer version
      exists, a link to a newer build, a setting that turns the
      opening by itself off, and the app's version shown anywhere but
      in the entries' headings (the model's version line, its 2.17,
      is not carried over).
    - iOS: nothing here is for Android alone, but no iOS build is made
      before PBI-1.23, so nothing is checked there.
12. **Tests** (**[Clead]**, 2026-11-26, #131), with the code, test
    first. Jest:
    - the list: not empty; each version a version (item 5), no two
      the same, in descending order; each date a real calendar date;
      in each language at least one line, none empty or only spaces,
      and the same number in the three; the version of `app.json` is
      the first entry's;
    - a version and the comparison: `0.9.0` below `0.10.0`; numbers
      longer than JavaScript's safe integers; a leading zero, a `v`, a
      space, two numbers, four numbers, a prerelease and a build part
      refused;
    - the decision of item 7 in each case: S missing, not a version,
      equal, higher, lower with one, several and no entry between,
      newest first; V unreadable and not a version;
    - the store: nothing stored (V written, nothing opens), a read
      that fails (nothing opens, the write tried), a write that fails
      (the decision still given), the stored text read back;
    - the order of item 8: nothing decided, shown or written before
      the disclaimer is accepted; after a new install's acceptance
      nothing opens and V is written; with a lower S the screen opens
      over the Library, and back shows the Library; at a start that a
      share opens nothing opens and nothing is written;
    - the screen: from the link, the title "Version history" and every
      entry; by itself, the title "What's new", the entries of item 7
      and the button; the button's press (the title, every entry, the
      button gone); the headings and the button as such to a screen
      reader; each of the three languages; the fallback of item 9; a
      line with `<` in it shown as text;
    - About: the link is first on the screen and opens the history.

    What only a phone shows is in Known unknown 17.
